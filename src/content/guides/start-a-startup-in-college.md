To start a startup in college, pick a real problem, talk to the people who have it before you build anything, and make the smallest version that tests your idea. College gives you cheap time, cheap labs and thousands of people to test with, so use them.

## Step 1: Start from a problem, not an idea

The best ideas come from problems you or your friends actually have. Write down five frustrations you saw this month. Pick one that people complain about often and already try to fix.

## Step 2: Talk to 20 people before building

Ask what they do today, what is annoying about it, and what they have tried. Do not pitch. If you cannot find people who care, change the problem before you spend months on it.

## Step 3: Build the smallest thing that can work

A landing page, a spreadsheet, a prototype, or doing it by hand for five customers. The goal is to learn quickly, not to impress anyone.

## Step 4: Find early users on campus

Students, clubs, labs and local businesses are a built-in test group. Offer your first version for free in exchange for feedback. Track who uses it again.

## Step 5: Use campus resources

Many schools offer entrepreneurship centers, competitions, mentors, maker spaces and sometimes small grants. Ask what exists. Check your school's rules about who owns work done with school resources, especially if it comes out of a class or lab.

## Step 6: Protect your grades and your time

Pick a weekly hour budget for the startup. Missing classes for a company that is still an idea is a bad trade. Many successful founders build while enrolled, in a steady and boring way.

## Step 7: Decide whether to form a company

You do not need a company on day one. Form one when you have customers, money coming in, or cofounders who need clear ownership. Get advice from a mentor or a campus legal clinic.
