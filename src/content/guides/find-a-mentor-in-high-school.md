To find a mentor as a high school student, choose someone a few steps ahead of you in your field, send a short and specific message, and ask for one small thing first. Mentorship usually grows out of a good first conversation, not out of the word "mentor" in the first email.

## Step 1: Decide what you want help with

"Be my mentor" is vague. "I am building a robotics club and want advice on recruiting members" is easy to answer. Write one sentence on what you are working on and one on where you are stuck.

## Step 2: Make a short list of people

Good places to look:

- Teachers and coaches who work in your field
- College students and recent graduates, who remember what you are going through
- Local professionals and business owners
- Founders and people who built something similar to what you are building
- Parents of friends, who can make warm introductions

## Step 3: Send a short message

Say who you are, what you are working on, why you picked them, and one specific question. Keep it under 100 words. Make it easy to say yes: "Could I ask you two questions over a 15 minute call?"

## Step 4: Come prepared

Have your questions written down. Send a thank-you note the same day, mention one thing you will try, and follow up a few weeks later with what happened. Following up is what turns a single chat into a mentor.

## Step 5: Keep it light

Do not ask for a job, money or an introduction in the first call. Let the relationship build. Share progress occasionally.

## Step 6: Have more than one

Different mentors help with different things. A teacher, a college student and an industry person each see your project differently.
