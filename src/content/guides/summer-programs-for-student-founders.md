To find a summer program for student founders, search for accelerators, fellowships and builder programs that accept your age and stage, then pick the one that gives you the most building time and the most useful people. Apply with something you have already started, because programs choose people who are doing, not planning.

## Step 1: Decide what you want out of the summer

Pick one main goal: build a product, get your first customers, meet mentors, or earn credibility. This decides which programs fit. A program that teaches theory will not help if you need to ship.

## Step 2: Know the types of programs

- **Accelerators and fellowships** for young founders, usually with mentors and a demo day
- **College-run entrepreneurship programs**, some open to high school students
- **Research and engineering programs**, if your project is technical
- **Nonprofit and community programs** that help you run something real
- **Do-it-yourself summers**, where you set milestones and find your own mentors

## Step 3: Check the details that matter

For each program, write down: who is eligible by age, cost, whether it is in person or virtual, time commitment per week, whether you must work on your own project, and what past participants did. Be skeptical of programs that charge a lot and promise outcomes without showing proof.

## Step 4: Check the deadlines now

Many programs close applications in winter or early spring. Put each deadline in a calendar and work backward.

## Step 5: Apply with proof

Show something you made: a working demo, users, a club with members, a project with a result. One clear page about what you built beats a long list of interests. Ask a teacher or mentor for a recommendation early.

## Step 6: Have a backup plan

If you do not get in, run your own summer. Pick a project, set weekly goals, book calls with people in your field, and show the result in September.
