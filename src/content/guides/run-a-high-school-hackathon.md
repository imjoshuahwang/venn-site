To run a high school hackathon, set a date and a theme, secure a venue and an adult supervisor, recruit participants and mentors, and keep the schedule simple. A first hackathon can be a single day with 30 to 50 people. It does not need to be huge to be good.

## Step 1: Decide the format

Choose in person, online or hybrid, and the length: 6 hours, a full day or a weekend. Smaller and shorter is easier to run well. Pick a theme or let people build anything.

## Step 2: Get permission and an adult

Talk to your school or a community space about the venue, insurance and supervision rules. You will need an adult on site. Ask early, because schools take time to approve events.

## Step 3: Set the budget

List costs: space, food, prizes, shirts, tools. Free or low-cost is fine. Local businesses, parents and tech companies sometimes cover food or prizes if you ask with a clear plan.

## Step 4: Recruit mentors and judges

Invite college students, local developers, teachers and professionals to help teams and judge. Send a short message with the date, time commitment and what you need from them.

## Step 5: Open registration

Use a simple form that collects name, grade, school, contact info for a parent and any dietary needs. Share it in clubs, group chats and with teachers. If participants are minors, get parent permission forms.

## Step 6: Plan the schedule

Opening, team formation, building time, a meal, check-ins, demos and awards. Leave time for questions and breaks. Keep the demo rules simple: two to three minutes each.

## Step 7: Write a code of conduct

Set clear expectations for behavior, safety and fair play. Share them before the event and have someone responsible for enforcement.

## Step 8: Run the day, then follow up

Assign roles for check-in, food, mentors and tech help. Afterward, send thank-you notes, share photos and collect feedback. Keep a record of attendance for your next event and your sponsors.
