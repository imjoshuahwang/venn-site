To partner with a college club, find a club in the same field as yours, email one officer with a small and specific idea, and offer something the club also wants. College clubs are run by busy students, so a short message with an easy yes works best.

## Step 1: Decide what you want to do together

Good first partnerships are small: a virtual session where members share what they build, a joint workshop, a competition your group can enter, or a mentor chat. Pick one idea and a rough date.

## Step 2: Find the right clubs

Search your local colleges' club directories and social pages for clubs in your field. Look at recent activity. A club that posted this month is more likely to answer than one that posted two years ago.

## Step 3: Find the right person

Look for the president, outreach chair or events lead on the club's page. Email one person, not the whole list.

## Step 4: Write a short message

Say who you are, what your group does, one thing you saw on their page, your one idea, and a yes or no question. Offer something in return: high school students who want to get involved, fresh members, or a project for their members to help on.

## Step 5: Make it easy for them

Offer to handle the details: scheduling, a meeting link, a sign-up form. Suggest a specific time and keep the first event short.

## Step 6: Run it well and say thank you

Show up on time, keep to the agenda, and share a recap afterward. A good first event is how you get a second one.

## Step 7: Respect the rules

If your members are minors, follow your school's rules on contact with college students, and keep any virtual sessions in a group setting with an adult informed.
