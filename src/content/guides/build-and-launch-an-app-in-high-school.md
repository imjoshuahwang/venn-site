To build and launch an app in high school, pick one small problem, build the simplest version that solves it, test it with about 10 real people and then publish. Most first apps fail because they are too big, not because the code is bad.

## Step 1: Pick one problem and one user

"An app for studying" is too wide. "A flashcard app for AP Biology vocabulary for my classmates" is buildable. Write who will use it, what they will do in the first minute, and what makes it worth opening twice.

## Step 2: Choose beginner-friendly tools

You can start with no-code tools, web apps (HTML, CSS, JavaScript) or a framework you can learn from free tutorials. A simple web app is the easiest to share: it needs no app store. Pick the tool that lets you finish in weeks, not months.

## Step 3: Sketch before you build

Draw the main screens on paper. List the three features that matter and cut everything else. A first version should do one thing well.

## Step 4: Build the core feature first

Make the one action work before adding accounts, settings or polish. Commit your work often and use a free code-hosting service so you do not lose anything.

## Step 5: Test with 10 real people

Hand your phone to a friend and watch them use it without helping. Note where they get stuck. Fix the top two problems and test again with new people.

## Step 6: Handle privacy and safety

If your app collects names, emails or anything from other minors, collect as little as possible, tell people what you store and ask a parent or teacher to review. Never store passwords or sensitive data unless you know how to protect them.

## Step 7: Publish and share

A web app can be live the same day. App stores have their own rules, fees and age requirements, so read them before you plan around one. Share it in clubs, group chats and communities where your user already is.

## Step 8: Keep a log

Track users, feedback and what you changed. This becomes the story you tell on applications, to mentors and to sponsors.
