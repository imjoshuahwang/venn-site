To scale a student startup, do a few boring things well and repeat them: serve one customer, charge early, write down what works, fill your gaps, watch one number, ask for introductions and stay alive long enough to compound. Here are seven things, in order. The first two are below; the rest unlock with a free account.

## 1. Pick one customer and serve them well

Scaling starts narrow. Choose one kind of person with one problem, such as first-year engineering students who cannot find a lab, and make them very happy before you widen. A small group that loves you tells friends. A large group that likes you does not.

Write the customer in one sentence: who they are, what they do today instead, and what it costs them. If you cannot write it, you do not have a customer yet.

## 2. Get paid, or get a real commitment, early

Money is the cleanest signal that the problem is real. If you cannot charge yet, ask for something that costs the person something: a deposit, a signed letter of intent, a weekly meeting on their calendar or an introduction to their boss.

Free users prove people will click. Paying or committed users prove people will care. Collect the second kind first, even if there are only three of them.
