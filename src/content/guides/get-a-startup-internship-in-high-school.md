To get a startup internship in high school, show a small piece of work, email founders at early-stage startups directly, and offer to help with one specific task. Startups care more about what you can do than your age, but they will not hunt for you.

## Step 1: Pick a skill to offer

Choose something useful: basic coding, design, social media, research, customer outreach or data entry. One clear skill beats a long list.

## Step 2: Make proof

Build a small project, run a club event, write a short case study or design a page. Put two or three examples on a simple page or document you can link.

## Step 3: Make a target list

Look at startups in your city or in a field you like. Small teams, often under 20 people, are more likely to take a student. Check their site for team pages and open roles, and follow them on social media.

## Step 4: Email the founder

Keep it short. Say who you are, what you noticed about the company, one thing you can do for them, and how much time you have. Example ask: "Could I help with research or outreach for five hours a week this summer?"

## Step 5: Be clear on work rules

Rules on unpaid work, work permits and hours vary by place and age. Ask your school counselor or check local labor rules, and get a parent involved. Paid, unpaid and volunteer arrangements are treated differently.

## Step 6: Treat the first month as a trial

Offer a small project with an end date. If it goes well, extend it. Ask for a recommendation or a reference at the end.

## Step 7: Keep a record

Write down what you did, what you learned and any results. This is useful for applications and your next internship.
