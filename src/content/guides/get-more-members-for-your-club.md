To get more members for your club, ask people in person, show them exactly what members do, and give new people a real role within their first two weeks. Most clubs do not have a recruiting problem. They have a first-meeting and follow-up problem.

## Step 1: Say what the club does in one sentence

"A club for robotics" is vague. "We build a robot every semester and enter two competitions" is a reason to join. Put it on every poster, post and sign-up form.

## Step 2: Ask people directly

Personal invites beat posters. Each current member should invite two friends. Ask in classes where you know people, and talk to teachers who might recommend you.

## Step 3: Make the first meeting a good one

Do something at the first meeting, not just introductions. Build, play, debate, design, or show a short demo. People return to clubs where they did something.

## Step 4: Capture contacts

Use a simple sign-up form with name, email or number and one question about what they want to do. Follow up within 48 hours with the next meeting time.

## Step 5: Show proof

Photos, a past project, a finished event or results from a competition tell people the club is active. Share them where students already look: group chats, social accounts and morning announcements, if your school allows.

## Step 6: Give new members a job

Members who have a role stay. Offer small starter tasks: running one meeting, taking photos, leading a team, bringing a guest. Within two weeks, every new member should own one thing.

## Step 7: Keep a rhythm

Meet at the same time every week. Add a goal with a date, like a showcase or a competition. People join clubs that are going somewhere.

## Step 8: Bring in outside energy

Guest speakers, college students and company demos make a club feel bigger. They are also a reason for someone to invite a friend.
