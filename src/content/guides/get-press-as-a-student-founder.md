To get press as a student founder, find a clear news angle, send a short pitch to local reporters and student publications, and give them facts, photos and a quote they can use. A small local article is a realistic first goal, and it often leads to bigger ones.

## Step 1: Find the angle

News is something that happened or is about to happen: a launch, an event, a result, a first in your town, a surprising number, or a local problem you are solving. "I started a company" is not news. "A junior built a tool used by 300 classmates" can be.

## Step 2: Gather the basics

Prepare a short description, a few facts with real numbers, two good photos, a quote from you and a contact. Put them on a simple page so a reporter can find everything.

## Step 3: Pick the right outlets

Start local: your school paper, the town paper, community newsletters, local TV and radio, college papers, and blogs in your field. Many reporters cover local students, and small outlets are easier to reach.

## Step 4: Write a short pitch

Three to five sentences: what happened, why it matters now, who you are and what you can offer, such as an interview or a demo. Send it to one reporter by name with a subject line that says the news.

## Step 5: Follow up once

Wait a few days, send a short follow-up and move on if there is no reply. Reporters get many emails.

## Step 6: Be easy to work with

Reply quickly, give accurate facts and never exaggerate. If you do not know something, say so. Ask for a parent or teacher to sit in on interviews if you are a minor.

## Step 7: Use what you get

Share the article, add it to your page and send it to mentors and sponsors. Press is proof that makes the next ask easier.
