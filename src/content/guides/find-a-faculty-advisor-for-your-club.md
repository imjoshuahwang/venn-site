To find a faculty advisor for your club, ask a teacher who already cares about your topic, tell them exactly how much time you need from them, and bring a short plan. Advisors say yes when the request is small, specific and well prepared.

## Step 1: Learn what your school requires

Check the rules: does the advisor have to be a teacher, a staff member or a coach? Does the school require a certain form? Some schools limit how many clubs one person can advise. Ask your activities office.

## Step 2: Think about who fits

Look for:

- A teacher in the subject your club is about
- A teacher you already have a good relationship with
- A coach or staff member who works with students outside class
- A teacher who has mentioned they like the topic
- Someone who attends school events regularly

## Step 3: Write a one-page plan

Include the club's purpose, what you will do at meetings, when and where you will meet, how many students are interested, and what you want from the advisor. Most advisors only need to be in the room or sign forms.

## Step 4: Ask in person

Visit during a free period or after class. Give a clear request: "Would you be able to supervise our club on Tuesdays at lunch? Here is the plan." Offer to handle the schedule, forms and attendance.

## Step 5: Make it easy to say yes

Say how long you need them. Give them the option to review the plan before deciding. Tell them you will run the meetings.

## Step 6: If they say no

Ask who else they would suggest. Teachers often say no because of schedule, not because of the idea. Try three or four teachers before changing your plan.

## Step 7: Keep your advisor happy

Send reminders, share updates and say thank you. Invite them to events. An advisor who sees the club working is more likely to stay for years.
