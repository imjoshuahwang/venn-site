To get sponsors for a high school club, decide exactly what you need money or product for, then ask a small number of businesses that already care about students. Keep the ask specific, small and easy to say yes to, and offer a clear way to thank them in public.

## Step 1: Get your school's permission first

Before you contact anyone, ask your advisor or activities office what the rules are. Some schools require approval for outside money, limit how a company's name can appear, or route funds through the school account. Finding this out first keeps you from losing a sponsor later.

## Step 2: Name the one thing you need funded

"We need money" gets ignored. "We need $300 for 20 T-shirts and snacks for our spring robotics showcase" does not. Write a one-line need, a cost, and a date. Sponsors like paying for something that has an end and a result.

## Step 3: Make a short list of likely sponsors

Start close to home:

- Local businesses your members already use
- Companies where a member's parent works (ask the parent to make the introduction)
- Companies that sell to students or build tools your club uses
- Local professionals such as dentists, real estate agents and tutoring centers, who often advertise to families

Aim for 10 to 15 names, not 100.

## Step 4: Write a one-page pitch

Include who you are, how many members you have, what you are doing, what it costs, and what the sponsor gets. Skip long histories. Attach photos from past events if you have them.

## Step 5: Offer something real in return

Logo on a banner or shirt, a thank-you post on your account, a mention at your event, or a short demo slot if their product fits. Only promise what you can deliver.

## Step 6: Ask in person or by email, then follow up

A short email or a quick visit works best. Ask for a specific amount. If you hear nothing in a week, send one polite follow-up. After they say yes, send a thank-you and a photo of the result.

## Step 7: Report back

After your event, send each sponsor a short summary: how many people came, what you did, a photo. This is what earns a second year of support, and it gives you a track record to show the next sponsor.
