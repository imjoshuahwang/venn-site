To write about your startup or club in a college application, pick one clear story, show what you did, say what changed because of it and describe what you learned. Specific facts beat big claims, and honesty beats polish.

## Step 1: Decide where it goes

Your activity can appear in the Activities section, an essay, an optional supplement or an interview. Do not repeat the same text everywhere. Use the activities list for facts and the essay for the story.

## Step 2: Gather your facts

Write down dates, hours per week, members, users, money raised, events held and results. Only use numbers you can back up. If you do not know exactly, use a range or leave it out.

## Step 3: Lead with the action

Start with what you did, not your title. "Built a tutoring program that matched 40 students with volunteer tutors" is stronger than "Founder and CEO."

## Step 4: Tell one story, not a résumé

Pick one moment: a failure, a decision, a conversation that changed what you built. Show how you thought and what you did next. A small honest story is better than a broad summary.

## Step 5: Show what you learned

Admissions readers care about how you grow. Describe a mistake, what it taught you and what you do differently now.

## Step 6: Show others

Mention the people involved: teammates, users, mentors, partners. Good projects usually involve others, and it shows you can lead and listen.

## Step 7: Avoid common mistakes

- Overstating impact or inventing numbers
- Using inflated titles
- Listing every task instead of the main result
- Writing about the idea instead of what you did

## Step 8: Keep proof

Save screenshots, event photos, emails of thanks and records of your work. A short portfolio page can help if a school allows extra materials. Check each school's rules before adding anything.
