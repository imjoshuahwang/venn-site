To pitch a company as a high school student, learn what the company cares about, make one small and clear ask, and send it to a specific person with a short message. Companies rarely say no to age. They say no to vague, long or irrelevant requests.

## Step 1: Know what you are asking for

Pick one ask: a product demo for your club, a small sponsorship, a guest speaker, free access to a tool, or feedback on your project. Write it in one sentence with a date and a number, such as "a 30 minute talk for our 40 members in March."

## Step 2: Research the company

Read their site and recent posts. Find what they build, who they serve, and whether they already work with students. Pick companies where your group is a natural fit, such as a coding club asking a developer-tools company.

## Step 3: Find the right person

A general inbox is a dead end. Look for someone in community, partnerships, marketing, university relations or recruiting. LinkedIn and the company's team page often show this. Small companies often let you write directly to a founder.

## Step 4: Write a short pitch

Four parts, in under 120 words: who you are, one real detail you noticed about them, what you want to do together, and what they get in return. State a clear question at the end that can be answered with yes or no.

## Step 5: Say what they get

Companies like clear visibility, honest feedback from real users, and a connection to people who may become customers or hires. Be concrete: how many students, what you will do, how you will share the result.

## Step 6: Follow up once, then move on

Wait about a week, send one short follow-up, then go to the next company. Keep notes of who you contacted.

## Step 7: Deliver and show proof

If they say yes, do exactly what you promised and send a short recap with photos or numbers. This is how a first yes becomes a repeat partner.
