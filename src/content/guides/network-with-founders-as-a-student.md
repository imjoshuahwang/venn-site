To network with founders as a student, go where they show up, send short and specific messages, ask good questions and follow up with what you did. Founders are usually happy to help a student who clearly did their homework and values their time.

## Step 1: Decide what you want to learn

"I want to meet founders" is too vague. Pick a question: how they found their first customers, how they chose a cofounder, how they got into a field. Specific questions get answers.

## Step 2: Find founders

- Demo days, pitch nights and startup events (many are free for students)
- Hackathons, where founders often judge or mentor
- Online communities and social posts, where founders share what they build
- Your school's alumni network and entrepreneurship centers
- Founders of products you use

## Step 3: Send a short message

Two to four sentences: who you are, what you saw about their work, your specific question and a small ask such as a 10 minute call or a reply by email. Do not ask for a job or money.

## Step 4: Come prepared

Read about their company before the call. Have three questions ready and keep to your time. Take notes.

## Step 5: Follow up with action

Send a thank-you the same day. A few weeks later, tell them what you tried because of their advice. This one step is what most students skip, and it is what makes people remember you.

## Step 6: Give something back

Share useful articles, introduce them to someone, try their product and give honest feedback. Networking works both ways, even for students.

## Step 7: Keep a simple tracker

A spreadsheet with names, dates, what you discussed and when to follow up is enough.
