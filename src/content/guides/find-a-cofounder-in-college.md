To find a cofounder in college, look for someone whose skills cover your gaps and whose working style you already trust, then test the fit with a small project before you commit. Most college cofounders meet through clubs, classes, hackathons and friends of friends, not through a "cofounder search".

## Step 1: Know what you are missing

List what your project needs: building the product, selling it, design, operations. Mark what you can do well. A cofounder should cover a real gap, not copy you. Two people who both want to do the product and neither wants to sell is a common trap.

## Step 2: Go where builders already are

- Startup and entrepreneurship clubs on your campus
- Hackathons, where you see how people work under pressure
- Classes with team projects in your target field
- Maker spaces, labs and demo days
- Friends of friends. Ask people you respect who they would work with.

## Step 3: Test before you commit

Do not start with a title or equity talk. Pick a two to four week project, such as a landing page, a prototype or a customer interview sprint. Watch how they handle deadlines, disagreement and feedback.

## Step 4: Check the hard questions early

Talk about time commitment, whether you both want to keep going after graduation, what happens if one of you gets an internship, and how you will decide when you disagree. It is easier to talk about these now than during a crisis.

## Step 5: Write it down

When it gets serious, agree in writing on roles, who owns what, and how any ownership split works. Campus legal clinics, mentors or a startup lawyer can help. Do not rely on a handshake once real money or users are involved.

## Step 6: Know that solo is fine too

You can start alone and add a cofounder later, or work with teammates who are not cofounders. A bad cofounder is worse than none.
